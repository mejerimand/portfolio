Imagination is an important site for change makers working for societal transitions. However, Anthony Dunne and Fiona Raby now argue that the design imagination remains heavily restricted by commitments to realism, plausibility, and futurity. This is a problem because, “... being possible simply ensures that something fits existing reality and reinforces it. A reality that for many people no longer works.” (Dunne & Raby, 2025, p. 17) This calls us to urgently find ways of engaging in radically different imaginaries that refuse techno-utopian escape routes and instead remain accountable to situated, earthly, and relational modes of existence. This project turned to fantasy as a potential pathway towards new possibilities for the design imagination.

The Arcane Designing Cards is a deck of prompting cards that supports playful imagination. They are designed to destabilise participants' ontological expectations of possibilities and foster ideas for radical alternatives. The deck consists of three categories: a fantastical ontological world condition, a mundane everyday practice, and a magical possibility. Together, these cards establish a structured yet open-ended system of worldbuilding. By using fantastical world conditions and magical possibilities, they help people's imagination depart from common assumptions about the future and move beyond restrictions put on the imagination.

The concept was tested with 22 designers in a workshop at ThingsCon, a conference with a focus on designing responsible technology. The findings affirm that fantastical worldbuilding can help designers move beyond modernity's constraints. The value lies not in fantasy's ability to magically solve problems, but in its capacity to estrange dominant reality, create permission for out-of-the-box thinking, and open affective and imaginative spaces where different ways of being, knowing, and relating can be explored.

The Arcane Designing Cards build on the game “The Thing From the Future” designed by Stuart Candy and Jeff Watson.
