In this design project, we asked questions about what design can do when the subject is something people would rather not name out loud.

Working with Copenhagen Pride Youth Group as our client, we used Social Implication Design to explore people's relationship to their own sexuality. Through participatory design, we discovered hundreds of context factors and collected them into eleven clusters: Internalised Beings, Taboo & Silence, Harmful Self-perception, Distressed Conservatives, A Realm of New Possibilities, and others. Through our analysis, we kept coming back to how the social constructs people carry internally do most of the damage. Although shame, low self-worth, and silence are definitely imposed from the outside, we found that the much bigger issue was how much of this was carried within, and how little focus there was on working with this. It is about the stories we tell ourselves, even if we do not know it.

Being the product-design engineering project this was, we were tasked with 'finding solutions' (although, looking back, this strategy is questionable). The design question shifted from being about how we can fix the problem into where can an object intervene in a norm a person doesn't know they are holding?

Through this project, we developed eight concepts, from which we further prototyped and validated two: Queergo, a block-based social game, and a Christmas calendar that smuggles small challenges to one's own assumptions in behind tips and prizes. This project did not conclude in a single concept, but the calendar was further developed beyond the project in collaboration with Peech and the Copenhagen Pride Youth Group.
