This Ritual, carried out in the honor of Ocean Spirits, is a speculation of a place-based community practice situated along the canals of a Copenhagen neighbourhood. Due to climate change, hordes of jellyfish have begun drifting into the canals each late summer, interrupting the swimming, kayaking, and sailing central to local leisure activities. The speculation reimagines these strange and ancient beings as ocean spirits, inspired by the landvættir of Scandinavian folklore, and proposes an annual ritual in their honour: knitted and crocheted jellyfish lanterns, crafted together by neighbours, families and school classes, and hung along bridges, railings, boats and trees so the water glows with colourful light. Rather than traditional food offerings, what is dedicated to the jellies is what the modern locals hold most scarce: time and attention, given through the slow, meditative labour of making-with. As people knit, they tell stories of these magical spirit beings on their slow passage around the globe, and talk about how to care for the waters they share.

The speculation's central finding is that by attending to trouble through the lens of folkloric practices we can sidestep the linear logic of problem-solving altogether: what might seem as an inconvenience to be eliminated becomes an occasion for reciprocity, and a human-centred “problem” is reframed as an opening for building caring, non-innocent relations with more-than-human others. Care here does not restore harmony or erase disruption but stays with the ambivalence of coexisting in damaged ecosystems, also where relations are strained.
