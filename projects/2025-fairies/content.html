The Feral Cyborg Fairies is a material fabulation that speculates on radically alternative AI. It draws on folklore to explore how we can imagine technology that embraces the hybrid messiness of our realities, refusing the choice between techno-deterministic narratives of the future and nostalgic fantasies of a more pure and 'natural' way of living. It was used to foster conversations with AI researchers at TU Delft.

One of the challenges in imagining alternative futures around technology, especially technologies as debated as AI, is how to support people in moving beyond dominant narratives. These, often reductive but strong, narratives prevent us from examining the underlying assumptions on which we design technologies. In response, this project engaged with folkloric practices as a design strategy for questioning current logics and proposing alternative relations between humans, technology, and ecological ecosystems.

The Feral Cyborg Fairies are small, mysterious hybrids of machine and organism that sense, respond, and act based on umwelts shaped by soil, plants, and fungi, as much as electronic sensors and circuits. Like fairies, they are mostly indifferent to human affairs unless we interfere with the ecosystems they inhabit. When harmed, they may respond mischievously, but when approached with kindness and respect, they may act with mutual care. Their behaviors embody the ambiguity and contradiction of more-than-human worlds, and they cannot be categorised with ethical dualisms of good and evil. Their feralness suggests a once-domesticated technology that has broken free from human control to live wild in natural ecosystems. As such, they are products of militarism, capitalism, and state power, creatures born out of anthropocentric systems, but they are not faithful to those origins. Instead, they exhibit unexpected desires to protect the planet and live in non-extractive entanglements with their surroundings.

As hybrids, they unsettle the binary between natural and artificial, and ask us to imagine forms of AI that participate in more-than-human worlds through ecological entanglement rather than human mastery. Drawing on the moral ambiguity of fairies in folklore, they provide a frame that avoids the dualistic tendency to see non-human agents as either resources to be exploited or fragile entities to be protected and left untouched.

The project draws on Donna Haraway's Cyborg Manifesto as a theoretical foundation.
