How might we make a different story about migration, one where people on the move are not treated as a problem to be managed but as fellow terrestrial beings we become with?

This project explores the future of migration, which is expected to grow as climate change and global instability make more places unlivable. The focus is on the overall narrative around migration at a systemic level, as this is a useful starting point for addressing complex societal challenges. The project uses the four keys from the Rockwool Foundation's Framework for System Innovation: purpose, relationships, resource flows, and power. To change a system, these keys must be unlocked by redefining them.

The model shows a possible transition narrative of migration to support a shift to a new system. On the left, we see the current system, and on the right, a new system. In the current system, the keys are locked tightly together. To understand the strong forces holding the system in this Gordian knot, we turned to what Arturo Escobar calls the dominant story of life, a story of separation, scarcity, competition, and control. Through this story, migrants are seen as a burden and a threat, and the purpose of the system becomes to contain them. Relationships run through the state, resources go toward control, and power structures are top-down.

On the right, the keys are redefined and loosened, now held together by a different story of life built on relationality, pluriversality, and autopoiesis. Here interdependence is recognised, and relationships grow between locals and newcomers, resources go toward healing, and power moves toward communal autonomy. The dotted line between the two circles is the path of transition. It will not be a fixed and clean path but an ongoing, messy process of learning to live well together on a strained planet of many worlds.
